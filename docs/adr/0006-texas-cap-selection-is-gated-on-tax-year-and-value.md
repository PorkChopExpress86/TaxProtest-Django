# Texas cap selection is gated on tax year and value

The shared cap-status evaluation selects a limit from the row's tax year and value as well as from its county cap flag. Tax Code 23.231's circuit breaker was added by Acts 2023, 88th Leg., 2nd C.S., Ch. 1 (S.B. 2) effective January 1, 2024, expires December 31, 2026, and qualifies only real property at or below the comptroller's published ceiling for the year ($5,000,000 for 2024, $5,160,000 for 2025, $5,320,000 for 2026), so `counties/common/cap_status.py` reports `cap_type="none"` for a non-homestead row in a tax year the section never governed and `cap_type="unknown"` for one whose value clears the ceiling, instead of attaching a 20% limit to it. The 23.23 homestead cap predates the 2022 start of the loaded history and carries no value ceiling, so it stays ungated. Because `AssessmentHistory` does not identify the owner's first qualifying tax year - the year 23.231(b) measures the ceiling against, after which qualification continues while that owner holds the property - the evaluator conservatively tests the current row's appraised value. This can leave a long-qualified property above the ceiling at "needs review", but it does not manufacture a 20% limit from a preceding-year value below the ceiling. The 23.231(f) ownership-tenure rule and the special-appraisal exclusions stay unmodelled because no field in the shared table reports them.
